Small Business Marketing Report 2026

Mike HawkesDirector of Local SEO Research
Oct 21, 2026•9 min read

On this page

  2. Accuracy is the quiet leak

  4. Reviews: valued, but rarely managed
  5. AI search is arriving faster than plans

  7. Limits of this survey

Every year we ask local business owners how they feel about their visibility on Google, and then we ask them to check. The 2026 edition surveyed 812 owners and managers of local businesses across the United States and Canada, from single-location trades to small multi-site operators. This report summarises what they told us and where the answers and the evidence disagree.

Method note. Findings come from our own online survey of 812 local business owners, fielded in September 2026. It is self-reported, not a statistically weighted national sample, so treat the numbers as directional rather than definitive.

The headline: confidence outruns evidence

The pattern was striking. Owners were sure they were doing fine, yet a basic self-check told a more mixed story.

0said they were confident their Google Business Profile is accurate
0found at least one wrong detail when they actually checked
0had experienced a suspension or verification problem
0do not reply to most of their reviews

Accuracy is the quiet leak

The most common errors were not dramatic. Owners found outdated opening hours, an old phone number on a directory, a closed second location still showing as open, or a wrong primary category. Individually these look minor. Together they erode the consistency signals that local ranking depends on, which we explore in our piece on map-pack ranking factors.

Who was most exposed

Businesses that had moved premises or rebranded in the last three years were far more likely to report a wrong detail than those that had not. Owners who had changed agencies were the group most likely to be unsure who actually owned their profile.

Suspensions are common, and costly

Nearly one in four respondents had faced a suspension or a stalled verification. Of those, most said they lost leads for more than a week, and a notable share did not know what had caused it. Those who had a documented evidence pack ready recovered noticeably faster than those who improvised, which matches what we see in client work. If this has happened to you, start with our recovery checklist.

Owners do not lack effort. They lack a way to see what Google sees.

Reviews: valued, but rarely managed

Almost every respondent agreed that reviews influence customers. Fewer had a routine for them. Most owners said they ask for reviews only when they remember to, and about two in three leave the majority of reviews unanswered. Negative reviews were the main source of anxiety, and a meaningful minority had received one they believed was fake but did not know how to challenge it. We cover that process in our fake-review guide.

AI search is arriving faster than plans

When asked whether customers would soon use AI assistants to find local services, a clear majority said yes. When asked whether they had done anything to prepare, only a small fraction had. The most common preparation was, tellingly, simply checking that their basic information was correct, which is exactly the right first step. See our note on AI-driven discovery for practical next moves.

What to do with these findings

  1. Audit before you assume. Spend thirty minutes comparing your profile, website and top directories line by line.
  2. Put one person in charge. Name an owner and a backup for the profile, with documented access.
  3. Build a review routine. A fixed weekly slot beats good intentions.
  4. Prepare for assistants. Accurate, consistent data is the cheapest preparation available.

Limits of this survey

Respondents were self-selected readers and customers, so they skew toward owners who already care about marketing. If anything, that makes the confidence gap more notable. We plan to repeat the survey next year with a larger and more varied sample.
