Map-pack ranking factors in 2026: what actually moves you into the top three

Mike HawkesDirector of Local SEO Research
Sep 10, 2026•11 min read

On this page

  1. Relevance: be clearly about one thing
  2. Proximity: the factor you cannot fully control
  3. Prominence: reputation, links and mentions
  4. Profile health: the factor people forget
  5. Engagement signals
  6. What does not move the needle
  7. A 90-day plan

The map pack, the group of three local businesses shown above regular results, drives a huge share of calls and direction requests. Google has long said local results are based on three broad things: relevance, distance and prominence. That framework still holds. What changes year to year is how much each part is worth in practice, and which tactics are noise.

Evidence note. The observations below come from our own client work and ranking grid tests across many local categories. They are patterns, not guarantees, and nobody outside Google knows the exact weighting.

Relevance: be clearly about one thing

Relevance is how well your profile matches the search. The strongest lever is your primary category. Choosing the most specific accurate category, such as "Emergency plumber" over a generic one, consistently produced the largest single gains in our tests. Secondary categories, services lists and a clear business description then fill in the detail.

  • Pick the primary category your best customers would search for.
  • Add only secondary categories that truly describe services you sell.
  • Fill in services and products with real descriptions, not keyword lists.

Proximity: the factor you cannot fully control

Distance between the searcher and your business is powerful, particularly for "near me" style searches. You cannot move your premises for rankings, but you can understand the effect. A ranking grid, which tests your position from points across your service area, shows where you are strong and where a competitor owns the neighbourhood. Many businesses discover they rank first at their door and nowhere three miles away.

Prominence: reputation, links and mentions

Prominence reflects how well known and trusted you are, online and off. In practice it blends reviews, links, citations and general web presence.

Reviews

Quantity, recency, average rating and the content of reviews all appear to matter. Reviews that mention services and neighbourhoods by name seem to help relevance too. A steady monthly flow outperformed a burst followed by silence, and replying to reviews is a cheap, positive signal. See our guide to handling fake reviews so one bad actor does not undo the work.

Citations and consistency

Matching name, address and phone data across the web is table stakes. It rarely lifts a strong listing, but inconsistencies can hold a good one back. Our cleanup playbook covers the process.

Website and links

A fast site with a dedicated page for each service and location, local links from real community partners and consistent branding supports everything above.

You do not beat competitors by doing one trick better. You beat them by having no weak spot.

Profile health: the factor people forget

A verified, active, policy-compliant profile is the entry ticket. Complete every field, add real photos regularly, keep hours accurate, use posts and answer questions. Profiles that were recently suspended or edited heavily often wobble before they recover, which is why we advise small, deliberate changes. If you are in that situation, start with the recovery checklist.

Engagement signals

Calls, direction requests, website clicks and photo views are strongly associated with higher visibility. We cannot say which causes which, but profiles that give people more reasons to engage, through photos, services, offers and quick answers, tend to climb.

What does not move the needle

  • Keyword-stuffing the business name. It can work briefly and often ends in suspension.
  • Fake addresses and virtual offices. A policy violation and a common suspension cause.
  • Buying reviews or links. Short-term bumps, long-term risk.
  • Constant edits. Frequent changes can look suspicious and reset trust.

A 90-day plan

  1. Weeks 1 to 2: fix the primary category, complete every profile field and correct NAP data.
  2. Weeks 3 to 6: launch a review routine, answer every review and add fresh photos weekly.
  3. Weeks 7 to 12: build service and location pages, earn a few genuine local links and re-run your ranking grid.
