How to legitimately remove fake reviews without getting flagged yourself

Kate Herbert-SmithHead of Suspension Recovery
Oct 8, 2026•8 min read

On this page

  2. Step 1: Build the case
  3. Step 2: Report it correctly

  7. Setting expectations

A fake one-star review from someone who has never been your customer can cost real money. It can also tempt otherwise careful owners into doing something that gets their own profile flagged. This guide covers the legitimate route: how to decide whether a review is removable, how to report it properly and how to protect yourself while you wait.

What Google will actually remove

Google removes reviews that break its content policies. It does not remove reviews simply because they are negative, unfair or from a customer you disagree with. Reviews that typically qualify include:

  • Spam and fake content, such as reviews from people who have no experience of the business, or posted in bulk.
  • Conflicts of interest, including competitors, former staff writing to harm, or reviews you or your team wrote yourselves.
  • Off-topic content, where the review is about politics, another business or a personal dispute.
  • Offensive or harassing language, including hate speech and personal attacks.
  • Restricted content, such as personal information or impersonation.
Honest test. Ask: "Does this review violate a written policy, or do I just dislike it?" Only the first is worth reporting. Reporting reviews that are merely unflattering rarely works and wastes goodwill.

Step 1: Build the case

Before you click anything, document why the review is not genuine. Useful evidence includes:

  1. Your records showing no customer, order or booking under that name.
  2. Screenshots of the review, the reviewer's profile and any pattern, such as a cluster of similar reviews on the same day.
  3. Proof of a conflict, for example the reviewer's profile showing they work for a competitor.

Keep this in a dated file. If the first report fails, evidence is what makes an escalation credible.

Step 2: Report it correctly

Use the flag option on the review itself and pick the policy it most clearly violates. Be precise: choosing "spam" for a review that is really off-topic weakens the case. If you manage many locations or a review is part of a pattern, use the dedicated review-management and redress channels available through your profile rather than repeatedly flagging.

Do not

  • Flag the same review again and again. It does not speed things up.
  • Ask friends and staff to mass-report. Coordinated reporting can itself look like manipulation.
  • Reply to the review with accusations. Your reply is public and permanent.

Step 3: Reply like a professional

Whether or not the review is removed, a calm reply helps. State that you cannot find a record of the visit, invite them to contact you with details, and keep it short. Future customers are reading the exchange, not just the complaint. A good reply often does more for your reputation than removal would have.

The best defence against one fake review is a steady stream of real ones.

The shortcuts that get you flagged

Owners under pressure sometimes try approaches that backfire badly.

  • Buying removal services that promise guaranteed deletion. Many use fraudulent mass-reporting and can put your listing at risk.
  • Posting fake positive reviews to drown the bad one. Review-gating and fake praise are policy violations and can trigger penalties or a suspension.
  • Offering incentives for reviews or for withdrawing a review. Both are prohibited.
  • Threatening legal action in public replies. It escalates the situation and rarely persuades the reviewer.

Step 4: Make fake reviews matter less

Volume and recency are your insurance. A business with 200 recent genuine reviews barely notices one outlier, while a business with 12 can drop a full star. Ask every happy customer, make it easy with a short link, and never pay or pressure. Reviews also feed ranking, so a healthy flow supports your map-pack position.

Setting expectations

Decisions can take days or weeks, and some legitimate reports are still declined. That is frustrating but normal. Keep your evidence, reply professionally, keep collecting real reviews and escalate through proper channels if the pattern continues.
