News that ChatGPT is introducing a business-facing hub, where companies can claim a profile and see how people interact with it, has local marketers talking. We will not pretend to know every detail of how any new product works, and you should treat any early claims, including ours, with care until the vendor documents them. What we can say with confidence is that assistants are increasingly being asked questions like "who is a good plumber near me", and the businesses that get named are the ones whose information is clear, consistent and well corroborated.

Our approach. This article is about preparation, not product specifications. Check the vendor's own announcement for current features, eligibility and terms before you act on anything specific.

Why this matters for local businesses

For two decades local discovery meant a list of links and a map. Assistants compress that into a short answer, often naming one to three businesses. Being in the answer matters far more than being on page one, and tools that show you how often you were surfaced or clicked would give small businesses something they have rarely had: visibility into the new channel.

What assistants appear to rely on

Assistants draw on a blend of sources: the business's own website, structured listings, review platforms, maps data and the wider web. Nobody outside those companies knows the exact weighting, but the inputs are familiar to anyone who has done local SEO.

Claim what you can claim

If a platform offers a way to claim or verify your business, take it, as long as the process is legitimate and uses your real details. Claiming gives you a say in how you are described and, where offered, access to analytics. Use the same exact name, address and phone number that appear on your Google Business Profile. A mismatch between sources is the easiest way to make an assistant unsure about you.

Get the foundations right

Whatever the platform, the preparation is the same work that helps you on Google today.

  • Accurate listing data. Hours, phone, address, categories and services, identical everywhere. Our citation cleanup playbook shows how.
  • A healthy Google profile. Assistants lean on maps and reviews data, so an unverified or suspended profile is a handicap. See our recovery checklist.
  • Genuine, recent reviews. Volume matters less than recency, specificity and honest replies from you.
  • Clear website content. Plain pages that state what you do, where you do it and for whom, with real photos and prices where you can.
Assistants recommend businesses they can describe with confidence. Your job is to make that easy.

Plan to measure, but stay sceptical

If a hub offers click or impression data, use it as one input, not as the truth. Compare it with calls from your Google profile, your website analytics and, most usefully, a simple question at the point of sale: "how did you find us?" Attribution in a brand-new channel is rarely clean, and early numbers can be misleading in either direction.

A ten-minute readiness check

  1. Search your business name and your main service plus your city, and note what is shown.
  2. Ask an assistant the way a customer would, and see whether you are named. Record the result with the date.
  3. Compare name, address and phone across your website, Google profile and top three directories.
  4. Read your latest ten reviews. Are they recent, specific and answered?
  5. Confirm you have at least two people with access to your key accounts.
